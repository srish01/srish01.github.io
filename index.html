Srishti Gupta

Postdoctoral Researcher, CISPA Helmholtz Center for Information Security, Saarbrücken, Germany

Machine learning models are tested in the lab but used in the world. I study what happens in between: how deployed models fail when their inputs drift, whether naturally or at an attacker’s hand, and how to detect, withstand and honestly measure that drift.

I work in Lea Schönherr’s group at CISPA. I completed my Ph.D. in Artificial Intelligence (summa cum laude) in the Italian National PhD Program, at Sapienza University of Rome co-hosted by the University of Cagliari, advised by Battista Biggio and Fabio Roli. Before research, I built data pipelines at JPMorgan Chase in New York.

Research

I work at the intersection of machine learning robustness and security, on how AI systems behave once deployed in open, evolving and adversarial environments. My work spans images, text, audio, code and malware.

Adapting to distribution shift

Detecting out-of-distribution inputs and learning continually without forgetting or full retraining, for vision, Android malware and deepfake detection.

Exposing failures of deployed AI

Adversarial and side-channel attacks, privacy leakage in memory-augmented LLM assistants and multi-agent systems, and trustworthiness gaps in audio language models.

Trustworthy evaluation

Uncovering methodological pitfalls in AI security research, auditing LLM-as-a-judge evaluations, and building generalization and robustness benchmarks.
